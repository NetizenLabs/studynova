# Fourteen Days Out, Finals Are Still a Choice

Two weeks before finals is the most useful distance a student can be. Seven days out, you're triaging. Thirty days out, everything feels too far away to start. But at fourteen days, you have enough runway to genuinely *build* — to cover the material properly, test yourself under real conditions, repair what's weak, and still arrive rested.

This plan divides the fourteen days into three phases with different jobs. It builds on the framework in [The Ultimate 7-Day Exam Revision System](/blog/exam-revision-system) and stretches it over two weeks. And if you're reading this with only a week left, don't despair — jump straight to our [7-Day Exam Revision Plan](/blog/7-day-exam-revision-plan), which compresses phases two and three into a single week.

Everything below assumes **two to four finals**. Scale the hours to your course load, but keep the phase order: the sequence is the strategy.

---

## The Three Phases at a Glance

| Phase | Days | Main job | Finished output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Build | 14–8 | Cover everything once, actively | Completed topic inventory with evidence-based confidence ratings |
| Prove & repair | 7–3 | Timed past papers, error logs, targeted repair | Marked papers, shrinking weak-spot list, realistic grade estimates |
| Taper | 2–1 | Light review, logistics, sleep | Short final-check pass, packed bag, protected nights |

Mark the phases on the [Exam Countdown printable](/printables/exam-countdown) — one row per day, with the phase, the day's main target and a confidence score. Seeing the countdown shrink is motivating; seeing your confidence column rise is better.

## Phase 1 — Build (Days 14–8)

**The job:** touch every topic once, in a way that produces evidence. Not "read Chapter 9" — *do* something with Chapter 9: work its problems, write its summary from memory, teach it aloud to an empty room.

**Day 14 is inventory day.** Before studying anything, list every topic for every final, and rate your confidence Red / Yellow / Green based on evidence, not vibes: Green means you have answered questions on it, unaided, correctly. If you have never produced an answer, it is not Green no matter how familiar the chapter feels when you reread it. This inventory drives the whole fortnight.

**Days 13–8 rotate through subjects** in focused blocks — two to three subjects per day, two to four quality hours per subject across the phase. Work in priority order: high exam weight × low confidence first. A topic worth 20% of the exam that you're Red on outranks everything else on the page.

Daily template for the Build phase:

- **Block 1 (deep):** New or weak topic — work problems, build notes, close gaps in understanding.
- **Block 2 (retrieval):** Self-test a topic covered 2–3 days ago. Spaced retrieval is what moves it into long-term storage.
- **Block 3 (light):** Flashcards, formula review, or outlining the next day's deep topic.

By the end of Day 8, every topic should have been touched and honestly rated. Some Reds will remain — that's exactly what Phase 2 is for.

---

## Phase 2 — Prove & Repair (Days 7–3)

**The job:** stop asking "do I know this?" and start asking "can I produce it, timed, on paper?" Those are different skills, and finals only test the second one.

- **One timed past paper per subject** across these five days, sat under exam conditions: no phone, no notes, full time limit. Mark it harshly.
- **Every error goes into an error log** with three columns: the topic, *why* it went wrong (didn't know it / misread / ran out of time / careless slip), and the fix. Patterns in the "why" column are the highest-value information of your entire countdown — three "ran out of time" errors is a pacing problem, not a knowledge problem, and it has a completely different fix.
- **Repair sessions target the log, not the textbook.** Re-study only what the papers exposed, then re-test that exact weakness within 48 hours.

Keep one retrieval block per day for your strongest subjects so they don't quietly decay while you repair the weak ones. Confidence ratings on your countdown sheet should visibly migrate Red → Yellow → Green during this phase; if a rating isn't moving after two repair sessions, change the method (problems instead of notes, teaching instead of flashcards), not just the effort.

---

## Phase 3 — Taper (Days 2–1)

**The job:** arrive rested with a brain that can access what it knows. Cramming here costs more than it buys.

- **Day 2:** One light pass over error logs and formula sheets per subject. A half-length timed set at most, only if it calms you. No new topics — a brand-new topic learned on Day 2 will not be retrievable on exam day, but the anxiety of meeting it will be.
- **Day 1:** Logistics and a short final check. Confirm each exam's time, room and permitted materials. Pack your bag. Lay out clothes. Set two alarms. Then close the books at a reasonable hour — the night-before hours are worth more as sleep than as study, because memory consolidation happens overnight, not at the desk.
- **Exam morning:** Normal routine, normal breakfast. A ten-minute skim of your highest-yield summary sheet if it settles you, then stop. You are not studying anymore; you are performing.

## Worked Example: Priya's Three Finals

Priya's finals: **Calculus (Mon)**, **Psychology (Wed)**, **Economics (Fri)** of finals week. Her countdown starts 14 days before the Calculus exam.

| Days | Calculus | Psychology | Economics |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14–12 | Inventory + weakest units (integration techniques = Red) | Inventory; key theories listed | Inventory; graphs & models rated |
| 11–8 | Build: problem sets per unit, retrieval rotation begins | Build: essay plans for major theories | Build: problem types drilled |
| 7–5 | Past paper #1 (timed) → error log: time management is the pattern | Past paper #1 → error log: two theory gaps | Build finishes; past paper #1 on Day 5 |
| 4–3 | Repair pacing: two half-papers, strict clock. Paper #2 | Repair theory gaps, re-test | Paper #2 → small repair list |
| 2–1 | Taper: error-log skim, early night Sun | Light retrieval of essay plans | Retrieval rotation continues (its exam is latest) |
| Finals week | **Mon: exam.** Evening off. | Tue: half-day repair. **Wed: exam.** | Thu: paper #3 + taper. **Fri: exam.** |

Two design choices do the heavy lifting. First, each subject's countdown is *offset* — Economics gets its heavy phase later because its exam is later, which keeps the daily load survivable. Second, Priya's evenings after each exam are genuinely off. Recovery between exams is part of the plan, not a reward for finishing it.

## Adapting the Countdown to Your Load

**One final only?** You don't need fourteen days of full intensity — compress: run Build in days 10–6, Prove & Repair in days 5–2, Taper on day 1, and take the extra early days genuinely off rather than inflating the plan to fill them. A plan padded to fill available time teaches you to work slowly inside it.

**Five finals, or finals on consecutive days?** Start the inventory two days earlier if you can, and lean harder on subject *rotation*: no subject goes more than two days without at least a short retrieval block, because decay doesn't respect your schedule. In Phase 2, interleave past papers (Calc paper Monday morning, Psych paper Monday afternoon) rather than finishing one subject completely before touching the next — your exam week will be interleaved whether you practise that way or not.

**Working a job during the countdown?** Halve the daily block count, never the phase structure. Two focused blocks a day for fourteen days, in the right order, beats six chaotic blocks for the final four days. Protect the inventory session on Day 14 and at least one timed paper per subject above everything else; those two produce the information the rest of the plan runs on.

---

## The Three Rules That Protect the Whole Countdown

1. **Sleep is a floor, not a variable.** Set a minimum (for most students, 7 hours) and treat it as untouchable from Day 14, not just Day 1. Every phase works worse on a sleep deficit; Phase 2's error data literally gets noisier.
2. **One real rest block per phase.** A half-day in Build, an evening in Prove & Repair. Fourteen days of unbroken grind reliably collapses around Day 10 — build the rest in before your brain schedules it for you.
3. **Evidence over hours.** Track topics moved to Green and errors repaired, not hours sat. Ten hours that produce no marked paper tell you nothing; the countdown sheet's confidence column is the scoreboard that matters.
